The Ultimate 3-Day Seoul Itinerary for First-Timers

By GoSeoul Editorial Team • Updated March 2026

Seoul is a massive, sprawling metropolis where ancient palaces stand literally in the shadows of glass skyscrapers. If you only have three days to experience the heart of South Korea, planning is essential. This itinerary is designed to minimize your transit time and maximize your experience, blending history, modern culture, shopping, and incredible food.

Day 1: The Historical Heart of Seoul

Your first day in Seoul should be dedicated to understanding the city's rich history. Jongno-gu, central Seoul, is where the ancient Joseon Dynasty established its capital over 600 years ago.

Morning: Gyeongbokgung Palace & Gwanghwamun Square

Start your day early at Gyeongbokgung Palace. Built in 1395, this is the largest and arguably the most beautiful of Seoul's five grand palaces. Arrive around 9:30 AM to beat the massive tour groups. If you want a truly immersive experience, rent a traditional Korean Hanbok from one of the many rental shops nearby; wearing one grants you free admission to the palace!

Be sure not to miss the Changing of the Royal Guard ceremony, which takes place at the main gate (Gwanghwamun) at 10:00 AM and 2:00 PM every day (except Tuesdays when the palace is closed).

Lunch: Tosokchon Samgyetang

A short walk from the palace is Tosokchon, famous for its Samgyetang (ginseng chicken soup). This rich, nutritious broth is a cultural staple and provides the perfect energy boost for a long day of walking. Expect a line, but it moves quickly.

Afternoon: Bukchon Hanok Village & Insadong

Next, walk over to Bukchon Hanok Village. This residential neighborhood boasts hundreds of traditional Korean houses called hanoks. Please remember to keep your voice down as actual residents live here. The winding alleys offer incredible photo opportunities, with views of traditional rooftops contrasting against modern high-rises in the distance.

Afterward, stroll down to Insadong. This main street is lined with antique shops, traditional tea houses, and art galleries. Stop by an Osulloc Tea House or a traditional spot to enjoy some matcha or Korean plum tea and a sweet rice cake.

Evening: Cheonggyecheon Stream & Myeongdong

As evening falls, take a walk along the Cheonggyecheon Stream, an urban renewal project that transformed a highway into a beautiful public recreation space. End your night in Myeongdong, Seoul's premier shopping district. Aside from endless skincare and cosmetics shops, Myeongdong is famous for its vibrant street food scene. Try the hotteok (sweet pancake), egg bread, and spicy tteokbokki directly from the carts.

Day 2: Trendy Culture, Art, and Youth

On your second day, you'll dive into the youthful, energetic, and trendy side of Seoul, focusing on shopping, modern art, and independent culture.

Morning: DDP (Dongdaemun Design Plaza)

Start at the futuristic Dongdaemun Design Plaza (DDP), designed by Zaha Hadid. The architecture alone is worth the visit. Inside, you'll find design exhibitions, unique shops, and modern art installations. It's a striking contrast to the palaces you visited on Day 1.

Lunch: Gwangjang Market

Just a short walk or subway ride away is Gwangjang Market, one of Korea's oldest and largest traditional markets. This is culinary heaven. You must try the bindaetteok (mung bean pancakes), mayak gimbap (mini seaweed rice rolls), and fresh kalguksu (knife-cut noodles). Grab a stool at one of the bustling stalls and soak in the chaotic, wonderful atmosphere.

Afternoon: Seongsu-dong (The Brooklyn of Seoul)

Take the subway to Seongsu-dong. Formerly an industrial shoe-making district, this area has been aggressively gentrified into Seoul's trendiest neighborhood. Old warehouses have been converted into massive, aesthetic cafes, pop-up stores, and art galleries. Spend your afternoon cafe-hopping (Onion Cafe is a popular choice) and browsing independent boutiques.

Evening: Hongdae

As the sun sets, head across the city to Hongdae (the area surrounding Hongik University). Known for its urban arts, indie music culture, clubs, and entertainment, Hongdae comes alive at night. Watch talented street performers (buskers) dancing and singing, shop for affordable indie fashion, and enjoy a classic Korean BBQ dinner paired with Soju and Somaek (soju mixed with beer).

Day 3: Modern Heights and the River

For your final day, cross the Han River to explore the modern wealth and lifestyle of southern Seoul.

Morning: Starfield Library & COEX

Begin your day in Gangnam at the COEX Mall. The centerpiece here is the stunning Starfield Library, featuring massive, 13-meter-tall bookshelves. It’s a photographer's dream. Browse the mall and enjoy a modern coffee in the heart of Korea’s business district.

Lunch: Gangnam Station Area

Head to the area around Gangnam Station. The streets behind the main road are packed with excellent dining options ranging from modern Korean fusion to premium pork belly BBQ. This is also a great place to observe the fast-paced lifestyle of modern Seoulites.

Afternoon: Hangang Park (Banpo or Yeouido)

A trip to Seoul isn’t complete without spending time by the Han River (Hangang). Rent a bicycle and ride along the scenic paths. A deeply ingrained local tradition is ordering fried chicken and beer (Chimaek) delivery directly to the park. If the weather is pleasant, sit on the grass, eat chicken, and watch the city flow by.

Evening: N Seoul Tower (Namsan)

Before you conclude your 72 hours, head back north to Namsan Mountain. Take the cable car up to the N Seoul Tower just before sunset. From the observation deck, you'll get a breathtaking 360-degree view of the entire city as it lights up for the night. It conceptually ties together the sprawling metropolis you've spent the last three days exploring.

Final Tips for Your First Visit

  • Public Transit: Get a Tmoney card immediately upon arrival at a convenience store. It works on all buses, subways, and even taxis.
  • Navigation: Google Maps does not provide walking directions in South Korea due to security laws. Download Naver Map or KakaoMap.
  • Connectivity: Rent a portable Wi-Fi egg or get an eSIM before you arrive. You will rely heavily on navigation apps.

Three days is barely enough to scratch the surface of Seoul, but by following this itinerary, you will experience a perfect cross-section of its history, modern culture, and incredible cuisine. Enjoy your trip!
